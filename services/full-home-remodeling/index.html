The short answer

A full home remodel changes several rooms and building systems under one scope, so layout, plumbing, electrical and finishes are planned and sequenced together instead of room by room.

Overview

What a full home remodel involves

A full home remodel touches more than finishes. Walls move, kitchens and baths are rebuilt, and electrical, plumbing and mechanical systems are often brought up to date at the same time. Because those trades overlap, the order of work matters as much as the design.

Golden Bay Builders approaches a full remodel as a single coordinated scope: a defined set of rooms and systems, a written sequence, and clear decision points for you along the way.

Common projects

Open the floor plan

Remove or reframe walls to connect kitchen, dining and living areas, including the structural work that requires.

Update an older home

Replace aging wiring, plumbing and finishes while keeping what is worth keeping.

Rework how the house lives

Move bedrooms, add a primary suite, create an office or improve flow between spaces.

Prepare a newly purchased home

Complete a larger scope before move-in, when the house is empty and access is easiest.

Homeowner concerns

What homeowners usually worry about

  • Living through construction

    Whether you stay or move out affects cost and schedule. We talk through both options before work is scheduled.

  • Surprises behind the walls

    Older San Jose homes can hide outdated wiring, plumbing or framing. Discussing allowances and how changes are handled up front prevents disputes later.

  • Decision fatigue

    A full remodel involves hundreds of selections. A clear selection schedule keeps orders ahead of the work that needs them.

Planning considerations

  • Define which rooms and systems are in scope, and which are explicitly not.
  • Decide early whether the design comes from an architect, a designer, or your own plans, since plans drive permits and pricing.
  • Identify structural changes first; they affect engineering, permits and schedule more than any finish choice.
  • Plan long-lead items (windows, cabinetry, custom tile) before demolition begins.

What helps us give an accurate estimate

  • Which rooms and systems you want to change
  • Existing drawings or photos of the home
  • Approximate age of the house and any known problems
  • Your target timeline and whether you will live in the home
  • A budget range, even a rough one

Questions

Full Home Remodeling: frequently asked

What counts as a full home remodel?

Generally, a project that changes several rooms and building systems under one scope, rather than a single-room update. There is no formal threshold; we define the scope with you in writing.

Do I need plans before I contact you?

No. Many homeowners start with goals and photos. If your project needs drawings or engineering, we can discuss how to obtain them.

Will I need permits?

Most whole-house work requires permits from the City of San José or the city where you live. Permit requirements depend on scope, and approval timing is set by the city, so we cannot promise a date.

Can I stay in my home during the remodel?

Sometimes. It depends on which systems are shut down and for how long. We discuss this before scheduling.
