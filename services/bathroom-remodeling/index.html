Bathrooms that are built to stay dry.

The finish you see is a small part of a bathroom. What lasts is the waterproofing, the framing and the ventilation behind it.

The short answer

A bathroom remodel combines plumbing, waterproofing, ventilation, tile and fixtures in a small space. The waterproofing behind the tile does more for longevity than the finish you see.

Overview

What a bathroom remodel involves

Bathrooms are small, but they combine plumbing, waterproofing, electrical, ventilation and tile in a very tight space. Errors in any one of them tend to show up later as leaks, mold or failed tile.

We plan the layout, specify the waterproofing system and sequence trades so each layer is inspected and finished before the next covers it.

Common projects

Primary bath remodel

Shower, tub, vanity and storage reworked together.

Tub-to-shower conversion

Replacing a tub with a walk-in shower for easier daily use.

Hall or guest bath refresh

New fixtures, tile and vanity in the existing footprint.

Bathroom addition

Adding a new bath where plumbing access allows.

Homeowner concerns

Practical considerations

  • Waterproofing

    Shower and tub areas need a proper membrane system. It is hidden when finished, so it is worth asking about.

  • Ventilation

    A correctly sized exhaust fan vented outdoors protects the room and its finishes.

  • Accessibility

    Curbless showers, grab-bar blocking and wider clearances are easiest to include during construction.

Planning notes

  • Keeping fixtures in their current locations keeps plumbing costs down.
  • Order tile, fixtures and glass early; custom shower glass is measured after tile is set.
  • Decide on niche, bench and storage locations before framing is closed.
  • Confirm the water heater and supply lines can support new fixtures such as rain heads.

What helps us give an accurate estimate

  • Photos and approximate size of the bathroom
  • Fixtures you want to keep or move
  • Any known leaks or water damage
  • Accessibility needs
  • Budget range and timing

Questions

Bathroom Remodeling: frequently asked

How long is a bathroom out of service?

Plan for several weeks for a full remodel, depending on scope and lead times. We give a schedule once the scope is defined.

Can you convert a tub to a shower?

Usually yes. It requires modifying the plumbing and floor, and we confirm the details on site.

Do bathroom remodels need permits?

Moving plumbing or electrical, or altering walls, typically does. Like-for-like fixture swaps may not. The city makes the determination.

Can you make a bathroom more accessible?

We can include features such as curbless showers, blocking for grab bars and wider clearances where the space allows.
