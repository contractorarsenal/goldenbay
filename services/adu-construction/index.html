An ADU (accessory dwelling unit) is a complete, independent home on a residential lot: detached, attached or converted from a garage. State law limits how cities can restrict them, but your lot determines what is practical.

Overview

What building an ADU involves

California law requires cities to allow accessory dwelling units on most residential lots, and San José publishes its own ADU guidance. What can be built on your lot, however, depends on its size, existing structures, utilities and zoning.

Because an ADU has its own kitchen, bath, entrance and utilities, it is closer to building a small house than to remodeling a room. Planning the site, utility connections and permit path early saves the most time and money.

Common projects

Typical work in this category.

  • Detached ADU

    A new stand-alone unit in the backyard. Offers the most privacy and design freedom, with the most site and utility work.

  • Attached ADU

    Built onto the main house, sharing a wall. Utility connections can be simpler than for a detached unit.

  • Garage conversion

    Converts an existing garage into living space. Uses the existing structure and slab, but the floor, insulation, egress and utilities still need work.

  • Junior ADU (JADU)

    A small unit created inside the existing home, with size limits set by state law.

Homeowner concerns

What to confirm before you commit

  • Size and setbacks

    State law sets floor-area and setback protections, but your lot’s layout determines what is practical. Verify with the City of San José.

  • Fees

    Some impact fees are waived for smaller ADUs, and larger units may be charged differently. The City’s fee page and the school district are the authorities.

  • Utilities

    Sewer, water, gas and electrical capacity can drive cost more than the structure itself.

  • Returns and rent

    We do not promise rental income or property-value gains. Those depend on the market and your circumstances.

How an ADU project moves

  • Feasibility: confirm your lot can host the type of ADU you want.
  • Design and engineering: produce permit-ready plans.
  • Permit submittal and plan review with the City of San José.
  • Construction: foundation, framing, systems, finishes.
  • Inspections and final sign-off before occupancy.

What homeowners say
Questions

ADU Construction: frequently asked

What is the difference between an ADU and a JADU?

An ADU is a separate dwelling on the lot. A junior ADU is a smaller unit carved out of the existing home, with a size cap set by state law and its own requirements.

Can I convert my garage into an ADU?

Often yes, but the garage must be structurally suitable and meet code for living space. We evaluate it before quoting.

How long does the City take to review an ADU?

State law sets a review deadline for complete ADU applications. Actual timing depends on how complete the submittal is and on corrections. See our San José ADU guide for the sources.

Can I add an ADU and split my lot under SB 9?

They are separate laws. SB 9 allows duplexes and lot splits on many single-family lots under specific conditions, and it can interact with ADU rules. Eligibility depends on zoning, overlays, tenant history and, for lot splits, an owner-occupancy affidavit. The City determines whether your lot qualifies; see our San José ADU guide for sources.

Are ADUs exempt from impact fees?

The City of San José states that park and school impact fees do not apply to ADUs under 750 square feet. Larger units are treated differently, and rules are changing, so confirm with the City.
