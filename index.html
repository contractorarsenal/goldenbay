How a project unfolds

The stages most residential projects pass through.

A general guide for homeowners. The scope, sequence and schedule for your own project are set in your estimate and contract.

  1. 01

    Goals and budget

    Define what you want to change, what the house allows and a realistic budget range.

  2. 02

    Site review and scope

    Walk the property, identify constraints and write down what is in and out of scope.

  3. 03

    Estimate and contract

    Compare written proposals on the same scope and settle terms before work begins.

  4. 04

    Plans, selections, permits

    Finalize drawings, order long-lead materials and obtain the approvals the work needs.

  5. 05

    Construction

    Demolition through finishes in a planned sequence, with decisions raised before they delay work.

  6. 06

    Inspections and closeout

    Required inspections, a final walk-through and completion of any remaining items.

Choosing a contractor

Six things worth asking any contractor.

Homeowners most often struggle with communication, cost changes and unclear scope. These questions apply to us as much as to anyone you interview.

Always check a license, bond and workers’ compensation on the CSLB license lookup.

Communication

Who is my point of contact, and how will I hear about progress and decisions?

A named person and an agreed routine, written into the contract, not a promise made in conversation.

Project Budget

What is included and excluded, what are the allowances, and how are changes priced?

A written scope with line-item clarity and a stated change-order process, with changes agreed before work happens.

Planning

What gets decided before demolition: selections, ordering, plans, permits?

Long-lead items identified early, and decisions scheduled so work is not left waiting on them.

Timelines

What does the schedule depend on, and what is outside your control?

Honest ranges that separate construction time from design, permitting and material lead times.

Quality

Which layers get inspected before they are covered, and who checks them?

Staged inspections and a clear walk-through at the end, with any punch-list items documented.

Permits and Inspections

Which permits does my project need, and who prepares and submits them?

A plain explanation of what the city requires for your scope, and no guarantee of approval dates.

Common questions

Before you call.

Short answers to what homeowners ask first. For more depth, start with the guides.

  • San Jose ADU guide
  • Remodeling budget guide
  • All FAQs
Where does Gold Bay Builders work?

San Jose, California is our home market. Additional nearby cities are being confirmed; ask us if your project is outside San Jose.

How do I start a project?

Request an estimate online, or use the Call Now button. We ask about your project type, location, timeline and budget range, then follow up to schedule a conversation.

Can you build from my architect’s plans?

Yes. We can also discuss what plans your project needs if you do not have them yet.

Can I stay in my home during a remodel?

Sometimes. It depends on which systems are shut down and for how long. We discuss this before scheduling.

Can I add an ADU and split my lot under SB 9?

They are separate laws. SB 9 allows duplexes and lot splits on many single-family lots under specific conditions, and it can interact with ADU rules. Eligibility depends on zoning, overlays, tenant history and, for lot splits, an owner-occupancy affidavit. The City determines whether your lot qualifies; see our San José ADU guide for sources.
